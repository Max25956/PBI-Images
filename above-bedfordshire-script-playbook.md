# Above Bedfordshire: Script Playbook

Evidence-based rules for writing scripts. Source: vidIQ analytics to 24 Oct 2026 for 9 public long-form videos (Apr–Sep 2026), retention curves for 6, transcripts for 6, plus one competitor (Wandering Northerner) for comparison.

**Confidence:** the rules below are correlations from 6–9 videos. They are strong enough to test, not proven. Treat each as an experiment and check the retention curve after publishing.

## 1. The problem in numbers

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Half of viewers leave in the first minute | 48–63% gone by the 10% mark (about 1 min) in every curve pulled. No improvement Apr → Sep. |
| Viewers give about 3 minutes whatever the length | Avg watch time 2:27–4:11 on videos of 6:34–13:20. |
| One video carries the channel | Wixams station: 14,676 views, 133 of 255 new subs (52%). Next best: 3,055 views. |
| Follow-ups lose the audience | Flitwick 652 → 298 → 69 views. Universal 14.7k → 1.5k. |
| Deliberate viewers stay twice as long | End-screen 62% and playlist 61% avg watched, vs 26.5% from feed. They are under 1% of views. |

## 2. Rules

**R1. Conflict plus outcome in the first 30 seconds.**
Winners: Flitwick ("5,500 objections… they still lost"), Wilstead ("The council hadn't refused anything yet"), Tempsford ("40,000 homes… a habit of changing history"). 10% retention: 50%, 51%, 52%. Weaker: Universal archaeology 37%, Wixams End 42%.
*Do:* first sentence states a conflict or paradox; the outcome is revealed by 0:30; one surprising number.
*Don't:* open on a site description or a date with no stakes.

**R2. No procedure before 1:30.**
Retention falls when the script moves from hook to procedure: Wilstead 72% → 51% between 0:28 and 0:58 ("outline application, access only, 9.4 hectares"). Wixams End has the application reference at 1:04.
*Do:* delay reference numbers, hectares, "outline application", and condition numbers until after the first story beat. Introduce them only when the viewer needs them.

**R3. Only promise what the video can answer.**
Wixams End opens "…said no. This is why." At 8:12 it admits the refusal reasons are unconfirmed. It has the worst start of all (82% at 1%, vs 92–96%).
*Do:* if the answer is unknown, say so in the first 30 seconds and make that the hook ("The council hasn't said why").

**R4. Fact-check the first line against the body.**
Wixams End line 1 says 345 homes and Central Bedfordshire Council. The title and body say 375 homes and Bedford Borough Council. Local viewers notice.
*Do:* check every number and authority in the first 30 seconds against the rest of the script and the title.

**R5. Give a roadmap, then pay it off.**
Tempsford: "three very different moments in history" (917, 1941, 2025). Retention recovers at 36–42% when 1941 arrives.

**R6. Re-hook about every 3 minutes.**
Retention rises at: Wixams 62–68% (the East West Rail myth correction: "most coverage has got this wrong"), Tempsford 36–42%, Wilstead near the end.
*Do:* place a surprise, a correction of a common belief, or a named person at each 3-minute mark.

**R7. Target 8–9 minutes.**
The last 40% of the 10–13 minute videos is watched by about 20% of starters. Longer than 9 minutes only if it earns a re-hook every 3 minutes.

**R8. Close with a question and a next step, and vary the sign-off.**
All videos use the same sign-off ("Subscribe so you don't miss the next visit…"). Only Tempsford and Flitwick ask a specific question. End screens are the best-retaining source, so point to the next video by name.

**R9. Sequels need their own conflict.**
Weak: "From Field to Foundations", "Show Homes: £529,950". Strong pattern: "The 5,497 objections didn't stop it. Here's the first home."

**R10. Names must be right on screen and in captions.**
Captions render the same developer as "Glad Group", "Clare Group" and "Glad room", and Wixams as "Wrexham". Upload corrected captions. Use one spelling (Wilstead or Wilshamstead) throughout.

## 3. Script template (9 minutes)

| Time | Section | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | Hook | Conflict or paradox, outcome, one number |
| 0:30–1:30 | Stakes | Who is affected; what is the open question; roadmap line |
| 1:30–3:00 | Context | Procedure and background, only what the story needs |
| ~3:00 | Re-hook 1 | Surprise, myth correction, or named person |
| 3:00–6:00 | Development | History, place or people between facts |
| ~6:00 | Re-hook 2 | Second turn or reversal |
| 6:00–8:30 | Resolution | What was decided, what it means, what the drone shows now |
| 8:30–9:00 | Close | Specific question, named next video, varied sign-off |

## 4. Topics and titles

- Winners combine a **named big project or place, a number, and a human or historical hook** (Wixams "£250m", Tempsford "40,000 homes"; WWII history in the three best long videos).
- Hyperlocal planning videos reach 280–300 views but have high share rates (Flitwick 3.1% of views shared). Treat them as community content, not growth content.
- Don't compete on weekly construction updates. Wandering Northerner has 10–22k views per weekly update and doesn't cover planning or the "why", which is where this channel is strongest.

## 5. Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Conflict and outcome stated by 0:30
- [ ] Nothing procedural before 1:30
- [ ] Every promise in the hook is answered in the video
- [ ] First-30-seconds facts match the title and body
- [ ] Roadmap line, with each item paid off
- [ ] A re-hook near each 3-minute mark
- [ ] Length 9 minutes or under, or re-hooks justify more
- [ ] Specific closing question and named next video
- [ ] Captions corrected; names spelled consistently

## 6. How to know it worked (check 7 days after upload)

| Metric | Baseline | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Retention at 10% mark | 37–52% | 55%+ |
| Avg watch time | 2:27–4:11 | 4:30+ |
| Avg % watched | 23–40% | 40%+ |

Not yet tested: whether the 8–9 minute target raises views, and whether the narrator's voice matters. Competitor audiences respond to a personal presenter; the transcripts can't show how the narration sounds.
